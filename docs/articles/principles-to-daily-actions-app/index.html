Loom workflow guidePrinciples and daily action

Turn your personal principles into visible daily action.

Loom helps you define direction in your own words, keep important life areas visible, and connect Goals with finite work, daily priorities, repeatable actions, and Reflection.

By Spencer DaughertyPublished August 12, 2026Updated August 12, 20267 min read

The short answer

Start with one principle in your own words, choose the life area it should influence, set a concrete Goal, define the next finite action, and select one small repeatable Little Win. Review what happened, then adjust the plan.

Loom is an AI-assisted life-management app for iPhone and iPad. It gives you a connected place for Purpose, customizable Fulfillment Areas, Goals, Projects, Action Plans, daily Musts, Little Wins, Capture, and Reflection. It does not tell you which principles to adopt or replace your judgment.

What is the difference between a principle, a goal, and an action?

LayerQuestion it answersExample
Personal principleHow do I want to make decisions?Protect time for the relationships and responsibilities that matter most.
Fulfillment AreaWhere in life should this stay visible?Relationships, Career, or Health & Energy.
GoalWhat concrete change do I want?Create a consistent weekly connection with family.
Project or Action PlanWhat finite result should I complete?Plan the next family visit.
MustWhat deserves priority today?Confirm dates with everyone involved.
Little WinWhat small action should repeat?Make one meaningful check-in each week.

Why can good intentions disappear from the daily plan?

Principles are broad by design. A calendar needs a time, a task list needs an action, and a habit tracker needs something repeatable. If the bridge between those levels is missing, important ideas may stay inspiring but absent from today’s decisions.

The solution is not to turn every value into a long list of obligations. A smaller bridge is often more usable: one relevant life area, one desired change, one finite next action, and—only when appropriate—one repeatable Little Win. Loom keeps those layers connected while letting each retain a different job.

Example: protect relationships during a demanding work season

  1. Write the direction in your own words. Add a Purpose statement that reflects how you want to balance achievement and connection.
  2. Keep the relevant areas visible. Review both Career & Business and Relationships rather than letting one replace the other.
  3. Choose a concrete outcome. Create a Relationships Goal that describes a change you can recognize.
  4. Define finite work. Use an Action Plan for something that can be completed, such as arranging a shared weekend.
  5. Select a repeatable action. Add one modest Little Win on a realistic schedule instead of creating an overwhelming routine.
  6. Review without self-punishment. Use Reflection to notice what happened and revise the next plan when work or life changes.

The structure cannot make the tradeoff for you. It keeps the tradeoff visible so you can decide again with better context.

When is a habit tracker or basic task list enough?

A focused habit tracker may be enough when you already know the behavior you want and only need a simple record of repetition. A basic task list may be enough when the work is clear and the larger context is already established elsewhere.

Loom becomes more relevant when you want to connect repeatable Little Wins with Goals, finite Action Plans, daily Musts, customizable Fulfillment Areas, and Reflection. It is intentionally broader than a habit-only or task-only tool, so people who prefer one narrow function may find a specialist simpler.

For more detail, see the guide to a habit tracker that connects actions with Goals and the comparison of Goals, habits, and tasks.

Who is Loom for—and where does it not fit?

Loom is for iPhone and iPad users who want their own direction, life areas, Goals, finite plans, daily priorities, repeatable actions, Capture, and Reflection in one connected personal system. Optional LoomAI can help generate or refine ideas, but the user remains responsible for choices and action.

Loom is not a course, philosophy, belief system, coaching program, medical or mental-health product, shared team workspace, Android app, browser app, or calendar-only planner. Apple lists compatibility with Apple-silicon Macs but notes that Loom has not been verified for macOS. It cannot guarantee personal, professional, or health outcomes.
