Comparison Goals and action

Goals, habits, and tasks do different jobs.

A goal describes a change. A task moves something forward. A habit supports progress through repetition. Mixing them creates clutter; connecting them creates a usable plan.

By Spencer Daugherty Published July 27, 2026 7 min read

The short answer

A Goal defines a meaningful outcome. An Action Plan is finite work that helps create it. A Little Win is a repeatable action that builds capacity or momentum over time. You may need all three, but they should not be managed as if they were identical.

Many productivity systems become difficult because every intention turns into a checkbox. “Run a half marathon,” “register for the race,” and “train three mornings a week” may appear together even though one is an outcome, one is a finite action, and one is a repeating behavior.

What a Goal is for

A Goal describes a future condition you care about creating. It should make the finish line clearer without pretending you already know every action required. A useful Goal has enough specificity to guide choices and enough meaning to survive a difficult week.

In Loom, Goals connect to broader Fulfillment Areas. That matters because the same Goal can look different depending on the part of life it supports. “Complete a professional certification” belongs to a different context than “restore enough energy to enjoy weekends,” even if both require scheduled work.

What an Action Plan is for

An Action Plan holds finite work: call the clinic, outline the presentation, submit the application, book the venue, or draft the first page. It can be completed, rescheduled, delegated, or removed.

Good actions make progress visible. They are specific enough that you can recognize when you have done them. Loom can add context such as a due date, duration, Must status, person, place, attachment, notes, and progress status.

What a Little Win is for

A Little Win supports progress through repetition. It is deliberately small enough to remain possible on ordinary days: walk after lunch, write for ten minutes, review the budget, pray, stretch, or call someone you care about.

Unlike a finite Action Plan, a Little Win is not meant to disappear after one completion. It builds evidence that an important area of life is receiving attention.

Example: preparing for a half marathon

  • Goal: Finish the October half marathon feeling prepared.
  • Action Plans: Register for the race, choose a training plan, buy suitable shoes, and schedule a physical if appropriate.
  • Little Wins: Complete the planned run, stretch for ten minutes, or protect a consistent bedtime on selected days.

The Goal keeps the outcome visible. Action Plans remove finite obstacles. Little Wins create the repeatable support that a single planning session cannot provide.

How to decide where something belongs

  1. Ask whether it has a finish line. If it describes a meaningful change, it may be a Goal.
  2. Ask whether one completion is enough. If yes, it is probably an Action Plan.
  3. Ask whether repetition is the point. If yes, it belongs in Little Wins.
  4. Connect all three to the same life context. That is what turns separate records into a coherent plan.

Do not force every action into a Goal

Groceries, household maintenance, and small obligations can be legitimate without serving a formal Goal. The purpose of context is to improve decisions, not to invent meaning for every errand. Use the lightest structure that makes the next choice clearer.
