Goals, tasks & habits

Goals vs. tasks: give each a different job.

A goal is an outcome. A task is a finite action. A habit is something you repeat. Knowing the difference helps you choose the work without losing sight of why it matters.

See one practical example

Three different jobs

Goal: what do I want to achieve?
“Pass a professional certification.” The outcome gives your effort direction.
Task: what can I complete?
“Complete the first practice test.” One completion is enough for this action.
Habit: what do I want to repeat?
“Study for ten minutes on selected weekdays.” Repetition is the point, rather than finishing it forever.

These can support the same part of life without becoming the same thing. A task does not need a grand goal to deserve a place in your plan. Booking an appointment or doing the dishes can simply be work that needs doing.

A worked example

Keep the goal. Make this week concrete.

Illustrative content, not a customer result or an app setup tutorial. This is one example block, not an entire weekly Action Plan.

Fulfillment Area: Career & Business.
Goal: Pass a professional certification.

One block in this week’s Action Plan

Result: Understand the first exam topic well enough to attempt a practice test.

  1. Read the first topic and note the points that are unclear.
  2. Complete a set of practice questions.
  3. Review the mistakes and list what needs another look.

If the practice questions are especially important to that result, you might mark that action as a Must. That is your judgment about importance, not an automatic ranking.

A repeatable support, kept separate

A Little Win could be studying for ten minutes on selected weekdays. It supports consistency; it does not replace the finite work above.

When you close the plan, Reflection helps you review the result and decide what needs to happen next. The goal can stay the same while next week’s work changes.

An Action Plan is not just another name for a task.

In Loom, Capture holds incoming actions. A weekly Action Plan organizes selected actions around results. A single action, a block and the whole weekly plan are different levels of that structure.

Choose what you need or want to do this week; leave the rest in Capture. Add detail when it helps the work, rather than treating every optional field as homework. A simple action can stay simple.

Common questions

Does every task need a goal?

No. Routine commitments and things you want to do can belong in Capture and your weekly plan without a formal Goal. Use the structure that helps you make the next decision.

When is a Project useful?

A Goal defines an outcome. A Project is useful when the work needs milestones with their own actions. The distinction is the structure you need, not simply how long something takes.

Are Little Wins another task list?

No. Little Wins are repeatable behaviors. Keep them distinct from actions that can be completed once, even when both support the same area of life.
