Independent fit guidePurpose-driven planning

Looking for a Tony Robbins RPM app? Start with the fit.

Choose official RPM software when you want to learn or apply the Rapid Planning Method. Consider Loom when you want an independent iPhone and iPad life manager that connects your own purpose, goals, plans, habits, daily priorities, and reflection.

By Spencer DaughertyPublished August 12, 2026Updated August 12, 20269 min read

The short answer

Loom is not the Tony Robbins RPM app and does not implement the Rapid Planning Method. Tony Robbins currently offers RPM Planner on the web and RPM Go as an iOS companion. Those official products are the right destination for RPM instruction, its proprietary planning structure, or Tony RPM AI.

Loom is a separate, independently designed life-management app. It can be a useful fit when the underlying need is to connect personally chosen Purpose, 3–7 customizable Fulfillment Areas, Goals, Projects, Action Plans, daily Musts, repeatable Little Wins, Capture, and Reflection on iPhone or iPad.

What should a purpose-driven planning app do?

A useful purpose-driven planning app should help you make four different decisions without collapsing them into one long list: what matters, what outcome you are pursuing, what finite work moves it forward, and what deserves attention today. It should also let you review what happened and change the next plan.

Loom uses its own product model for those decisions. Purpose records the direction you choose. Fulfillment Areas keep the important parts of life visible. Goals define desired changes. Projects and Action Plans organize finite work. Musts identify today’s priorities. Little Wins hold small repeatable actions. Capture receives loose inputs, while Reflection supports the next adjustment.

Example: launch an offer without losing the rest of life

Imagine that you want to launch a consulting offer while protecting health and relationships during a demanding month.

  1. Define your own direction. Write a Purpose statement in your own words and choose the Fulfillment Areas that need attention.
  2. Create the desired outcome. Add a Career & Business Goal with a clear finish line.
  3. Organize the work. Use a Project for milestones and Action Plans for finite next steps.
  4. Choose today’s priority. Mark one action as a Must instead of treating every open action as equally urgent.
  5. Protect repeatable progress. Add realistic Little Wins in Health & Energy or Relationships, then use Reflection to adjust.

This is a Loom workflow, not a restatement of RPM. It shows Loom’s released structure and leaves the decisions with the user.

Where Loom fits—and where it does not

Loom is a strong fit for iPhone and iPad users who have outgrown disconnected task, habit, goal, notes, and journal apps and want one whole-life planning structure. LoomAI can provide contextual suggestions, but the user reviews and chooses what to use.

Loom is not RPM software, a Tony Robbins course, coaching, therapy, or a substitute for human judgment. It is not available on Android or the web, and it is not a calendar-only planner or shared team-project workspace. If any of those needs is central, another product is the better choice.

How is this different from a basic to-do list?

A to-do list can store work without showing the desired outcome or the part of life it supports. Loom keeps finite actions connected to Goals, Projects, and Fulfillment Areas, while Little Wins remain separate from one-time work. That structure is useful when checking more boxes does not answer whether the right part of life is moving.
